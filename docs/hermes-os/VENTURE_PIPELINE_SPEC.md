# Venture Pipeline Specification

## Purpose

Define the standard lifecycle every venture must follow within the Master Venture Portfolio.

The objective is to move ideas from concept to revenue using a repeatable process.

No venture may skip stages without explicit Command Center approval.

---

# Stage 1 — Ideas

## Purpose

Capture opportunities.

## Owner

venture_portfolio

## Entry Criteria

* New business idea exists.
* Problem statement identified.

## Deliverables

* One paragraph description.
* Initial customer hypothesis.
* Initial revenue hypothesis.

## Exit Criteria

* Idea is worth researching.

## Possible Outcomes

* Move to Research.
* Kill.

---

# Stage 2 — Research

## Purpose

Determine if the opportunity is real.

## Owner

venture_portfolio

## Deliverables

* Market overview.
* Competitor analysis.
* TAM estimate.
* Customer profile.

## Exit Criteria

* Evidence of demand exists.

## Possible Outcomes

* Move to Validation.
* Kill.

---

# Stage 3 — Validation

## Purpose

Confirm people actually want the solution.

## Owner

venture_portfolio

## Deliverables

* Validation plan.
* Customer interviews.
* Landing page test.
* Demand evidence.

## Exit Criteria

At least one of:

* Prospective customer interest.
* Waitlist signups.
* Pilot customer.
* Pre-sale.
* Strong demand signals.

## Possible Outcomes

* Move to MVP Design.
* Return to Research.
* Kill.

---

# Stage 4 — MVP Design

## Purpose

Define the smallest useful solution.

## Owner

venture_portfolio

## Supporting Profiles

* engineering_lab
* consulting_office
* engineering_brand

## Deliverables

* MVP scope.
* Features list.
* Success criteria.
* Timeline.

## Exit Criteria

* Clear implementation plan exists.

## Possible Outcomes

* Move to MVP Build.
* Return to Validation.

---

# Stage 5 — MVP Build

## Purpose

Build the smallest version capable of testing the market.

## Owner

engineering_lab

## Deliverables

* Working MVP.
* Documentation.
* Test results.

## Exit Criteria

* MVP functional.
* Ready for pilot customers.

## Possible Outcomes

* Move to Pilot.
* Return to MVP Design.

---

# Stage 6 — Pilot

## Purpose

Test the MVP with real users.

## Owner

venture_portfolio

## Supporting Profiles

* consulting_office
* engineering_brand

## Deliverables

* Pilot users.
* Feedback.
* Metrics.
* Lessons learned.

## Exit Criteria

* Evidence of customer value.

## Possible Outcomes

* Move to Revenue.
* Return to MVP Build.
* Kill.

---

# Stage 7 — Revenue

## Purpose

Prove repeatable revenue generation.

## Owner

venture_portfolio

## Deliverables

* Paying customers.
* Pricing model.
* Revenue tracking.

## Exit Criteria

* Consistent customer acquisition.
* Repeatable sales process.

## Possible Outcomes

* Move to Scale.
* Return to Pilot.

---

# Stage 8 — Scale

## Purpose

Increase growth and operational capacity.

## Owner

command_center

## Supporting Profiles

* engineering_lab
* consulting_office
* engineering_brand

## Deliverables

* Growth plan.
* Automation plan.
* Hiring plan (if required).
* Expansion roadmap.

## Exit Criteria

* Sustainable growth.

---

# Stage 9 — Killed

## Purpose

Archive ventures that do not justify continued investment.

## Rules

* No emotional attachment.
* No sunk-cost bias.
* Resources must be reallocated.

## Deliverables

* Kill summary.
* Lessons learned.

---

# Venture Scorecard

Every venture receives a score from 1–10 in:

* Market Demand
* Revenue Potential
* Ease of MVP
* Competition
* Strategic Fit
* Personal Interest

Maximum Score: 60

## Guidance

50–60
Pursue aggressively.

40–49
Investigate further.

Below 40
Kill unless strategic value exists.

---

# Core Principles

1. Validate before building.
2. Revenue before scale.
3. Kill weak ideas early.
4. Favor recurring revenue.
5. Favor leverage over labor.
6. Every venture must move a KPI.
7. Every venture must belong to a stage.
8. Every venture must have a next action.
